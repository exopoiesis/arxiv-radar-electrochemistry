The imprint of neutron escape on non-thermal protons in AGN coronae

Antonio Ambrosone, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo

arXiv:2611.21047·astro-ph.HE·Published 2026-11-16

The coronae of active galactic nuclei (AGN) are promising sites of proton acceleration to hundreds of TeV, potentially accounting for the neutrino excess from NGC 1068 reported by IceCube. We show that photohadronic proton-to-neutron conversion provides an important proton escape channel, as neutrons can leave the corona well before decaying. In magnetized turbulent coronae, neutron escape can reduce the characteristic neutrino energy by up to a factor of four and the integrated neutrino luminosity by approximately 30% at a fixed non-thermal proton energy density. More strikingly, proton-to-neutron conversion offers an escape route roughly two orders of magnitude faster than proton diffusion, requiring a correspondingly larger proton injection rate to sustain the non-thermal population. The effect is considerably weaker when proton escape is already ballistic, as expected in magnetic-reconnection scenarios. Therefore, neutron escape is essential for quantitatively connecting neutrino emission from AGN coronae to the efficiency of proton injection into the acceleration process.
