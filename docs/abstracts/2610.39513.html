Distilling Large-Scale Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials to Predict Long-Timescale Electrolyte Transport Properties

Ishan Amin, Yue Jian, Nitesh Kumar, Christine Zhang, Jianwei Lai, Casey S. Mezerkor, Aaron M. Melemed, Jonathan R. Thurston, Neil P. Dasgupta, J. David Bazak, Ethan J. Crumlin, Samuel Blau, Aditi S. Krishnapriyan

arXiv:2610.39513·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-10-29

Accurately predicting the transport properties of liquid electrolytes is critical for next-generation battery design and requires force fields that are both quantum-mechanically accurate and fast enough for long-timescale simulation. Recent large-scale pretrained machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approach density-functional-theory accuracy, but remain too slow to access the necessary timescales to converge transport properties. We provide a knowledge distillation procedure to transfer the accuracy of a large-scale "teacher" MLIP into fast, system-specific "students", without any additional ab initio data. Our distilled models run ~40x faster, reproduce teacher-predicted structural observables, and produce stable 20 ns trajectories in days, rather than the months the teacher would require. Across a diverse set of sodium- and lithium-ion electrolytes, student-predicted self-diffusivities, ionic conductivities, and shear viscosities track experiment and outperform classical force-field predictions, establishing distillation as a practical route to accurate, long-timescale simulations necessary for high-throughput electrolyte screening.
