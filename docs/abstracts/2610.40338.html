Differentiable Electronic Grand Canonical Learning for Electrochemistry

Phillip E. Pope, Timothy C. Berkelbach, Venkat Kapil

arXiv:2610.40338·physics.chem-ph·Published 2026-10-29

Electronic grand-canonical density functional theory captures how structure and electronic charge respond to electrode potential and, together with statistical mechanics, how these responses give rise to emergent phenomena. However, its computational cost limits the system sizes and sampling times accessible to first- principles simulations. Here, we introduce a differentiable architecture that represents the grand potential and obtains forces and electronic charge as its derivatives, exactly satisfying the mixed-derivative constraints required for thermodynamic consistency, and implement it within the equivariant MACE framework as GC- MACE. We demonstrate more accurate joint learning across multiple electrochemical interfaces and improved transferability to thicker water films relative to previous models, while enabling constant-potential simulations at substantially larger length and time scales than accessible to ab initio molecular dynamics. With broad training data, the framework offers a route to thermodynamically consistent foundation interatomic potentials for the electronic grand canonical ensemble
