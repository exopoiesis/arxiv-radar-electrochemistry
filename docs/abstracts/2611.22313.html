Supercell periodicity biases octahedral tilting and energetics in high-entropy perovskite oxides

Hanzo Tsubota, Wai Kian Tan, Hiroyuki Muto, Atsunori Matsuda, Tom Ichibha, Kenta Hongo, Ryo Maezono, Liangzhi Kou, Go Kawamura

arXiv:2611.22313·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-11-17

Odd-period supercells such as $3 \times 3 \times 5$ are often used for equimolar five-component compositions in computational studies of high-entropy perovskite oxides (HEPOs). However, such cells are known to constrain octahedral tilting because they cannot accommodate all standard tilt patterns (Glazer tilt systems). How strongly this constraint affects HEPOs, whose octahedra respond to local cation environments, remains unclear. Here we show that supercell periodicity systematically biases computed octahedral tilting and energetics. Using a machine-learning interatomic potential, we compared odd-period and tilt-commensurate cells for 13 HEPOs. Odd-period cells yielded smaller and less coherent tilts and higher energies, even after configurational averaging. In ~1,700-atom cells, tilt differences persisted in all compositions and energy differences in most. The bias extended to defects, with lower oxygen vacancy formation energies in odd-period cells for strongly tilted compositions. Based on these findings, a simple design rule for equimolar supercells is proposed to lift this constraint.
