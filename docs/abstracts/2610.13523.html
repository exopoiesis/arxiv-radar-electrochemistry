How Fluctuations Control Chiral Symmetry Breaking in Reacting Mixtures of Chiral Molecules

Leonardo Silva-Dias, Christoph A. Weber

arXiv:2610.13523·cond-mat.soft·Published 2026-10-09

The pronounced chiral asymmetry of biomolecules is a defining feature of living systems, yet the mechanisms by which such asymmetry emerged remain unresolved. Chiral symmetry breaking may have occurred under prebiotic conditions in systems with sufficiently small molecular populations for intrinsic fluctuations to play an important role. Here, we investigate how finite-population fluctuations affect chiral-state selection, the characteristic time for symmetry breaking, and stochastic switching between chiral states in a system driven by autocatalysis and mutual antagonism. We determine how these dynamical processes scale with particle number and derive an analytical expression for the probability of selecting either chiral state as a function of the initial enantiomeric bias. We further calculate the mean selection time for different initial biases and show that, for weak mutual antagonism, fluctuations can induce transitions between opposite chiral states. These results characterize how finite-population fluctuations control both the emergence and stability of molecular chirality in autocatalytic chemical systems.
