A Science Traceability Matrix for Micro-arcsecond Resolution X-ray Imaging

Jenna M. Cann, Kimberly A. Weaver, Ryan W. Pfeifle, Jeffrey D. McKaig, Teresa Monsue, Laura D. Vega, Ronald Gamble, Miranda McCarthy, Kyla Mullaney, Kelly E. Whalen, Erini Lambrides, Atul Mohan, Isabella Carlton, Emma Kleiner, Kaylee DeGennaro, Mainak Singha, Andrea Astorga Bedoya, Neshini Sathishkumar

arXiv:2611.21770·astro-ph.IM·Published 2026-11-16

Our ability to understand the highest energy processes and environments in astronomy is directly tied to our ability to probe their physical processes at the smallest scales. Despite transformative progress in high-angular resolution instruments across the radio through optical, angular resolution within the X-rays has plateaued around $\sim0.1-1''$, and therefore our window into the innermost regions of the high energy Universe lies primarily within spectral, timing, and/or polarimetric studies rather than direct imaging. This represents a significant barrier to gaining a robust understanding of key science questions, including the mechanisms of accretion and jet launching, the accretion activity and angular momentum losses of binary active galactic nuclei (AGN) leading to gravitational wave events, and the space weather events that may influence atmospheric evolution and prebiotic chemistry on potentially habitable exoplanets. The Accretion Explorer Interferometer (AEI) is a mission concept for X-ray imaging that could achieve up to $μ$as resolution, $\sim5-6$ orders of magnitude better than current instrumentation. Here we present the scientific discovery space enabled by such an advance, and highlight areas of uncertainty and open questions that are critical to investigate for future development of this concept.
