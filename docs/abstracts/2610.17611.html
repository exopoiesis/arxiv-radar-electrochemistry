A grand potential for molten-salt electrochemical phase diagrams from first principles

Lorenzo Bastonero, Lucio Colombi Ciacchi, Nicola Marzari

arXiv:2610.17611·cond-mat.mtrl-sci·Published 2026-10-13

The clean-energy transition is driving unprecedented demand for metals, whose extraction is among the largest industrial sources of carbon emissions. Molten-salt electrolysis offers a path toward carbon-free production of aluminum, iron, titanium, and critical metals. However, corrosive operating conditions and complex, multicomponent feedstocks make experimental development costly and challenging. Here, we introduce a first-principles framework for constructing electrochemical phase diagrams of multicomponent systems to accelerate electrode and electrolyte design and guide the selection of compatible materials and operating conditions for electrochemical cells. We derive a grand potential that generates these diagrams at scale, and supply free energies using first-principles data obtained through a fine-tuned foundation machine-learned interatomic potential. For silicon in molten CaCl$_2$, the construction not only recovers the experimental diagram when using the first-principles framework on a limited set of structures, but also predicts further domains when extended to a larger set of crystal structures. Finally, we demonstrate the predictive power of the approach by screening ten alkali and alkaline-earth halide electrolytes for aluminum electrowinning. Three thermodynamic criteria identify NaF, where cryolite emerges as the stable aluminum-bearing phase, recovering the chemistry that Hall and Héroult established empirically in 1886. The framework provides a first-principles basis for selecting compatible electrodes, electrolytes, and operating conditions toward low-carbon metal production from complex source materials.
