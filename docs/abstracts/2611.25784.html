Quantitative approximations for finite-state mean-field configuration models

Arnab Ganguly, Yangrui Xiang

arXiv:2611.25784·math.PR·Published 2026-11-19

We establish quantitative approximations for the empirical measures of finite-state mean-field particle systems in a general framework. The scales of the empirical drift and covariance distinguish the ODE and critical diffusion regimes and determine the corresponding time rescaling. In the ODE regime, we obtain quantitative central limit approximations in the $1$-Wasserstein distance and a Bernstein-type concentration inequality. The concentration bound has no dimensional prefactor. Its explicit time dependence also identifies growing intervals on which the ODE approximation remains accurate. In the critical regime, we prove a quantitative nonlinear diffusion approximation in the $1$-Wasserstein distance. We apply our quantitative approximation results to noisy voter systems, Moran models with mutations and selections, heat-bath Glauber dynamics for the $p$-spin Curie--Weiss model, and autocatalytic reactions with mutations.
