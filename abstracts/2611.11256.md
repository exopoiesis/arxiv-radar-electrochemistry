# Engineering ferroelectric nanocapacitors through interfacial phonon bias

**Authors:** Iñigo Robredo-Magro, Urmimala Dey, Suehyun park, Karam Cho, Raseong Kim, Jorge Íñiguez-González

As ferroelectric devices approach the nanometer scale, their behavior becomes increasingly sensitive to boundary conditions. In perovskite oxide nanocapacitors, epitaxial strain and electrostatic screening are well-established control parameters. Then, while expected to contribute to the epitaxial stabilization of ferroelectric ultrathin films, the lattice-dynamical state of the electrodes has not been isolated as an independent tuning knob. Here we call this poorly explored coupling interfacial phonon bias, whereby the electrodes' phonon energy landscape reshapes that of the adjacent ferroelectric across their shared interface. Using first-principles-accurate machine-learned interatomic potentials and molecular-dynamics simulations, we examine this mechanism in symmetric BiFeO${}_3$ nanocapacitors in contact with a metallic perovskite oxide. By independently controlling the orientatio and amplitude of the electrodes' octahedral-tilt landscape, while keeping conventional elastic and electric boundary conditions fixed, we show that the electrodes' phonon energy landscape can modify the stability of competing BiFeO${}_3$ phases. In particular, this allows us to shift the ferroelectric transition temperature and even induce antiferroelectric-like switching at ambient conditions. We thus showcase interfacial phonon bias as a complementary and powerful strategy to modify and optimize the properties of ultrathin epitaxial films.
