# Atomistic simulations of electrochemical systems with machine learning potentials

**Authors:** Marialore Sulpizi, Jörg Behler

Modern machine learning potentials (MLPs) enable large-scale atomistic simulations at unprecedented accuracy. The incorporation of explicit physical interactions such as long-range electrostatics has continuously broadened their applicability to increasingly complex systems in chemistry and materials science. Extending MLPs to electrochemistry and electrocatalysis, however, is still challenging as it requires the consideration of electrode potentials and electric fields in the atomic interactions. Such approaches have emerged only recently, and although the field is evolving rapidly, further conceptual advances are still needed for carrying out reliable simulations at finite temperature under applied potential. In this review, we provide a comprehensive overview about recent progress in the atomistic simulation of electrified interfaces using MLPs. Current developments fall into three different categories. The first comprises linear response models that describe polarisation, polarisability, and Born effective charges, extending conventional MLPs to finite electric fields. The second includes methods that directly learn a field-dependent potential energy surface. The third consists of a series of hybrid approaches combining classical models with machine learning. Although still under active development, these methods represent a major step toward more realistic simulations approaching the quality of electronic structure calculations. Finally, we discuss open challenges and possible future directions for advancing MLP-based simulations aiming for a bottom-up understanding of atomistic processes at electrified interfaces.
