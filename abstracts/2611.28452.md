# A Covalent Bonding Network Using p-Block Elements to Stabilize Lithium-Rich Cathode Materials

**Authors:** Amelia Zhao, Edward Mu, Jessica Luu, John Vinson, Brian Moritz, Eder G. Lomeli, Thomas P. Devereaux

Li-rich battery cathodes (LixMyOz, where x > y) have sparked significant attention for their high energy densities, but their practical application is currently limited by structural instabilities associated with high-valent redox. Recent work has demonstrated reversible high-potential (de)lithiation with low voltage hysteresis in Li4FeSbO6 (LFSO), but the stabilization mechanism for this unique charging behavior is not fully understood, limiting our ability to apply insights from LFSO to more earth-abundant cathodes. In this paper, we present a covalent Sb-O bonding network in LFSO which is facilitated by the p-block orbital character of Sb. Using ab initio computational modeling, we identify this bonding state and indicate its contributions to crystal stability; we also identify a signature for this bonding network using theoretical and experimental element-specific X-ray spectroscopy. Substituting Sb with other p-block elements preserves this stabilizing bond, pointing to a family of new cathode chemistries beyond LFSO. This work establishes a design principle for development of sustainable energy-dense batteries: p-block elements like Sb possess specific orbital-mediated oxygen bonding which contributes to stability of high-valent layered oxide cathodes.
