# Axial Thermal Transport in Transition Metal Chalcogenide Nanowires from Machine-Learning Molecular Dynamics Simulations

**Authors:** Wenwu Jiang, Hekai Bu

Transition metal chalcogenide M6X6 nanowires (M = Mo or W; X = S, Se, or Te) are promising one-dimensional (1D) building blocks for nanowire bundles and arrays, but how elemental composition controls their intrinsic axial heat transport remains unclear. Here we develop a multicomponent neuroevolution potential (NEP) from first-principles reference data and use homogeneous nonequilibrium molecular dynamics (HNEMD) simulations to quantify the thermal conductivity of six MX6 nanowires. The Mo6X6 series show a nonmonotonic composition dependence, whereas the thermal conductivity of the W6X6 series decreases monotonically with increasing chalcogen mass. Frequency-resolved thermal conductivity and phonon mean-free path analyses show that axial heat conduction is dominated by low-frequency phonons. Vibrational density of states and spectral energy density analyses further reveal that the contrasting trends arise from a competition between metal-chalcogen vibrational matching and phonon softening induced by heavier chalcogen atoms. A phenomenological model incorporating the combined atomic mass and the relative mass difference between the metal and chalcogen atoms reproduces these trends. These results link elemental composition, phonon characteristics and axial thermal conductivity in M6X6 nanowires, providing an atomistic basis for selecting heat-conducting units in nanowire bundles and arrays.
