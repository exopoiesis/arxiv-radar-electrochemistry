# Oxygens breathe, electrons jump

**Authors:** Cristiano Malica, Nicola Marzari

Electronic transport in polyanionic battery cathodes proceeds through small-polaron hopping between transition-metal sites and is commonly assumed to be tightly coupled to the motion of charge-compensating mobile ions. Using olivine Li$_x$MnPO$_4$ as a prototypical platform, we use first-principles molecular dynamics with extended Hubbard functionals to follow, in real time and at finite temperature, the adiabatic evolution of Mn oxidation states and atomic sublattices. We find that polaron hopping and cation migration are dynamically decoupled: Mn$^{2+}$/Mn$^{3+}$ valence exchanges occur frequently while the intercalated ions remain confined to their sites, and hopping is gated by the local oxygen framework. Freezing the six oxygen atoms of an individual MnO$_6$ octahedron excludes that site from the hopping network, while increasing the fictitious oxygen mass slows down electronic transport. Each valence exchange coincides with a concerted breathing of the donor and acceptor octahedra ($ΔV \simeq 2$ $Å^3$), with their volume asymmetry crossing zero at the transition, consistent with the Holstein-Marcus coincidence condition. The hopping rate yields an activation energy of $E_a = 0.20 \pm 0.03$ eV, closely matching the elastic energy scale of the two coordination cages ($λ_V/4 \approx 0.20$ eV). These results identify the oxygen coordination cage, not the mobile cation sublattice, as the dynamical gate for small-polaron transport: a site can accept the electron only if its cage can breathe, and the barrier is governed by cage elasticity. Selective constraints and mass scaling applied to first-principles dynamics provide a general route to link electronic and lattice degrees of freedom in redox-active materials, opening the door to framework engineering in intercalation and mixed ionic-electronic conductors.
