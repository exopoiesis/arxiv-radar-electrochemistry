# Fine-tuned atomistic foundation model for accurate sodium-carbon chemistry in Na-ion batteries

**Authors:** Andres Felipe Usuga, Ihor Radchenko, Yuxin Tang, Oleksandr I. Malyi

Atomistic foundation models have emerged as a promising route to DFT-level accuracy for materials simulation with complex chemical environments. However, their reliability across competing bonding regimes and, more importantly, how complex bonding affects predictions of material properties remain unclear. Among materials with competing bonding regimes, sodium storage in hard carbon poses a substantial challenge. Hard carbon features diverse local structural motifs, including defective and curved graphitic regions, amorphous regions, and closed/open pores. Motivated by its importance for Na-ion batteries, we systematically evaluate existing pretrained foundation models based on the MACE architecture across different local Na/C motifs. We find that the pretrained models fail to accurately capture Na-Na and Na-C interactions for metallic Na clusters on graphene. To recover a DFT-level energetic description, we employ targeted fine-tuning, showing that it can describe the energetic stability of local motifs and achieve sufficient transferability to unseen Na/C environments. With the developed fine-tuned model, we establish a correlation between closed-pore radius in hard carbon and plateau voltage, showing that the small pores contribute at larger voltages than large ones, suggesting thus how the developed fine-tuned foundation model can link atomistic information with materials design for complex bonding environments.
