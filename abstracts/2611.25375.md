# Weakly coordinating anions and solvents break the conductivity-transference-solvation tradeoff

**Authors:** Alex Liu, Ganesh Raghavendran, Eric S. Yang, Kevin J. Anderton, Vibhu Vardhan Singh, Aiden Larson, Kun Ryu, Kent J. Griffith, Joshua S. Figueroa, Ying Shirley Meng, Tod A. Pascal

Lithium-metal batteries promise high energy density, but common electrolytes trade ionic conductivity against the share of current carried by lithium ions and against how strongly solvent binds those ions. We show that simultaneously weakening both interactions of the lithium ion, with solvent and with anion, relaxes this trade-off. Thus, a weakly coordinating perfluorinated alkoxyaluminate salt in liquefied fluoromethane conducts 39.2 mS cm$^{-1}$ at 25 $^\circ$C while maintaining a lithium-ion transference number of 0.71. Molecular dynamics simulations further reveal that the anion prevents persistent ion aggregation and lowers the salt's dissociation penalty. Consistent with these properties, nickel-rich cathode/lithium-metal anode full cells charge at 10C at room temperature and at 2C at $-40$ $^\circ$C. We advance coordination strength as a design axis for future energy storage devices.
