# Symmetry-Induced Conservation Laws, Three-Channel Attribution, and Solid-Phase Recycling in Prebiotic Enantiomeric Excess Amplification

**Authors:** Zeyu Jia, Anlin Gong, Bingmei Wu

Enantiomeric excess ($\mathrm{ee}$) is the primary observable used to quantify chiral amplification across prebiotic chemistry, asymmetric autocatalysis, and multiphase crystallization. Because $\mathrm{ee} = Δ/ M$ is the ratio of net chiral excess $Δ= L - D$ to total chiral mass $M = L + D$, identical increases in $\mathrm{ee}$ can arise either by generating net chiral excess through parity-breaking reactions or by depleting total chiral mass through symmetric phase or oligomer partitioning. Here we establish a pathwise conservation law and a model-free three-channel attribution identity for $\mathbb{Z}_2$-symmetric reaction networks. In any closed network without autocatalytic feedback or epimerization, net chiral excess is conserved along every stochastic trajectory ($Δ(t) = Δ(0)$), locking liquid-phase amplification to cumulative mass loss ($\mathrm{ee}_f = \mathrm{ee}_0 M_0 / M_f$). Re-analyzing seven experimental systems shows that standalone phase removal (Channel R) loses $91.7\%$--$98.9\%$ of chiral solute into racemic sediment in closed batches and washes out under open racemic inflow. Coupling eutectic partitioning (Channel R) with solid/solution racemization recycling (Channel B) converts the precipitated solid into homochiral product ($96.5\%$ recoverable homochiral crystal mass at $96.5\%$ total and $100.0\%$ solid $\mathrm{ee}$), whereas coupling evaporative concentration with liquid-phase autocatalysis crosses the open-flow bifurcation threshold against continuous racemic dilution ($81.9\%$ homochiral yield at $92.9\%$ active chiral $\mathrm{ee}$). We further establish non-crossing parity invariants for dual-monomer networks, derive a closed-form wet--dry amplification law, and resolve a one-dimensional identifiability ridge in chiral mineral adsorption via antipodal crystal-pair protocols.
