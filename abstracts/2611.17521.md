# Thermally Activated Magnetic Switching in Antiferromagnetic Van der Waals Trilayers Resolved by Single-Spin Diamond Magnetometry

**Authors:** Fabian A. Gerritsma, Roland W. Mulder, Balázs Nagyfalusi, Pieter C. J. A. van Zweeden, Samer Kurdi, Francisco Machado, Jaime Ferrer, Samuel Mañas-Valero, Toeno van der Sar

Van der Waals magnets provide a versatile platform for exploring magnetic phenomena that emerge from reduced dimensionality, including layer-dependent magnetic ordering. Here, we use scanning nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond magnetometry to measure the magnetization of a trilayer of the A-type antiferromagnet CrPS$_4$, resolving its zero-field bistability between the up-down-up ($\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow$) and down-up-down ($\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow$) layer magnetization configurations. The coercive field decreases approximately linearly with temperature, consistent with the Néel--Arrhenius model. Density Functional Theory calculations capture the temperature dependence of the magnetization and determine the coercive field in the coherent-rotation case. The measured coercive fields are lower, identifying thermally activated domain nucleation as the reversal mechanism. Spatial magnetic imaging supports this picture, revealing independently switching regions likely pinned by structural inhomogeneities. Our results highlight the power of scanning NV magnetometry for the quantitative investigation of magnetization reversal in two-dimensional magnets, unraveling the magnetic phenomenology that emerges in low-dimensional systems.
