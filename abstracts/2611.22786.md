# Quantum-classical dynamical density functional theory for solvation dynamics

**Authors:** Paul Bergold, Irene Burghardt, Francesco Di Maiolo, Cesare Tronci

We provide a unified modeling framework for thermalization and non-equilibrium spectroscopy in overdamped polar environments. This framework extends dynamical density functional theory to describe the coupled quantum-classical evolution of a molecular electronic subsystem and an overdamped polar solvent represented by a reaction-field coordinate. The nonadiabatic dynamics is formulated in terms of an operator-valued distribution obeying a quantum-classical Ehrenfest--Smoluchowski equation which exhibits nonlinear classical diffusion and non-increasing free energy, thereby retaining detailed balance. In contrast, a quantum-classical Liouville--Smoluchowski model with quantum-classical linear diffusion develops unstable long-time behavior and fails to reach a stationary thermal state. The method is applied to a two-level model of a polar dye in three different solvents: chloroform, dichloromethane, and dimethyl sulfoxide, each differing in polarity and dielectric relaxation time. Absorption and time-resolved emission spectra are obtained from dipole-dipole correlation functions, capturing the solvent-induced spectral broadening, the dynamic Stokes shift, and the time evolution of the emission bandwidth. The calculated solvent spectral response agrees well with the experimental relaxation trends of the three solvents.
