# Hydration Free Energies from Local Electronic Structures

**Authors:** Kasper F. Schaltz, Jonas Greiner, Janus J. Eriksen

Free energies of solvation are crucial to our understanding of chemical reactions, particularly when these take place in water. However, computing changes in free energies associated with the process of immersing arbitrary molecules in an aqueous solution both accurately and efficiently with the use of one universal method remains challenging. By means of atomic decompositions of total (bulk) energies from Kohn-Sham density functional theory in a basis of spatially localized molecular orbitals, the present study enables the computation of hydration free energies from local electronic-structure objects derived in the condensed phase. Specifically, our protocol computes an enthalpy of hydration as the sum of the decomposed change in internal energy of a solute upon solvation and a newly developed term that accounts for the response of the environment, which may then be combined with a standard expression for the corresponding change in entropy. In applications to eight neutral solutes in water, internal energies are all found to converge rapidly and in a smooth manner upon an increase in the size of the solvated cluster, and the corresponding enthalpies and free energies of hydration are likewise found to agree with experimental references within statistical uncertainty.
